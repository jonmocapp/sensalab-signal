Honda folding its cars into Encanto fandom at D23 signals the product as scene

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

Honda and Disneyland turned their two-decade alliance into an Encanto activation at D23 2026, from a character dance experience to vehicles staged inside the fandom. The signal is the product placed inside the story, not beside it.

At D23: The Ultimate Disney Fan Event, held 14 to 16 August 2026, Honda and Disneyland built their footprint around Walt Disney Animation Studios' film Encanto rather than a conventional vehicle stand, according to Event Marketer. Guests could join an Encanto Dance Experience, dancing alongside characters from the film, then download and share an exclusive D23 video, and every attendee received a set of commemorative D23 buttons featuring the movie's characters. Honda's vehicles, including the Odyssey minivan and Acura's flagship three-row MDX, were staged inside that themed environment rather than lined up on a plinth. The activation extended a partnership that Honda dates to January 2005, which has spanned the sponsorship and re-engineering of Disneyland's Autopia attraction and fireworks sponsorships, and under which Honda is the official vehicle of the Disneyland Resort.

The signal is the product inside the story

A car company at a fan expo could default to a row of vehicles and a spec sheet. The signal in Honda's approach is the inversion: the draw is the Encanto experience, and the product lives inside it. That reframes the vehicle from the thing being shown to part of the scene the audience chose to enter, and it trades passive proximity, a shopper walking past a display, for participation, a fan dancing with a character and leaving with a video and a button. The brand's payoff is not a glance but a memory of taking part, which is a far stickier form of attention at an event built entirely around fandom.

For a producer, the transferable discipline is to lead with the reason to engage and let the brand sit inside it. That means designing the interaction first, what a visitor does, feels and takes away, and then placing the product where it belongs in that moment, rather than starting from the asset you must feature and bolting an experience around it. The shareable video and the collectible button are worth noting too: they give the guest a role and a takeaway, turning a booth visit into something the visitor carries out of the room and, often, onto their own feeds.

Why it belongs in an experiential toolkit

Honda's D23 build is a clean example of a brand borrowing an existing story's emotional pull instead of manufacturing its own from scratch, the same logic behind any licensed or co-branded activation. The craft is in the integration: making the product feel native to the world rather than an interruption of it, and giving guests an action, not just a sight. A long-time sponsor choosing a themed, participatory experience over a static display is a reminder that relevance at a fan event is earned by joining the story the audience came for, and designing the brand's place inside it deliberately.

Your takeaway

Put the product inside the experience, not next to it: give visitors a reason to engage with the story first and let the brand live inside that scene, so people remember taking part rather than passing a display.

Why it matters

When a long-time sponsor turns its expo presence into a themed experience built around a film rather than a vehicle stand, it signals that the effective move is to place the product inside a story the audience already loves, earning attention through participation instead of proximity.
