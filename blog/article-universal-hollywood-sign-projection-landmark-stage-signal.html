Universal projecting onto the Hollywood Sign signals the protected landmark as a one-night stage

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

Universal projection-mapped the Hollywood Sign to launch its Fast & Furious: Hollywood Drift ride, working with Paintscaping and the Hollywood Chamber of Commerce. The signal is a protected landmark becoming a licensed, temporary screen.

Universal Studios Hollywood projection-mapped the Hollywood Sign to mark the opening of its Fast & Furious: Hollywood Drift roller coaster, which opened on 16 September 2026, according to AV Magazine. The 14-metre-tall white letters were showered in chrome and lit with imagery of speeding-car lights drawn from the films, with the words Fast & Furious appearing across the sign's W. The mapping was carried out by Los Angeles-based studio Paintscaping, with technical support from Jeff Kleiser, a visual-effects veteran and chief executive of Synthespian Studios, whose property sits directly below the landmark. Universal created the promotion in partnership with the Hollywood Chamber of Commerce, the body that stewards the protected sign.

The signal is permission, not spectacle

The eye-catching part is the chrome, but the real signal is access. The Hollywood Sign is a protected landmark that is normally off-limits as an advertising surface, so the story is less about the projectors than about the deal that lit them: a studio, a mapping specialist and the chamber that controls the site agreeing to turn a civic monument into a screen for one night. The canvas already carried a century of recognition; the work was unlocking it, not building it. That is a different production model from fabricating a set, and it puts the heaviest lifting into rights, siting and a projection plan rather than steel and freight.

For a producer, the transferable move is to scout before you build. The most efficient path to reach and recall is frequently a place the audience already knows, converted into a temporary stage through the right permissions and a careful projection design, rather than a structure raised from nothing on a rented lot. That means starting with who controls the surface, what the sightlines and throw distances allow, and how a short, well-documented activation earns its coverage, then designing the content to the landmark instead of forcing a generic set into an unfamiliar space.

Why it belongs in an experiential toolkit

Projection mapping onto an existing structure is one of the highest-leverage tools an experiential team has, because it borrows a location's built-in meaning and scale instead of paying to manufacture them. The craft is technical, aligning content to an irregular real-world surface, and it is logistical, since a landmark activation lives or dies on permissions, timing and a clean single-night execution. Universal's choice to route the launch through the sign's steward, rather than a purpose-built stage, is a reminder that the site and its rights are part of the creative brief, not a detail handled after the concept is locked.
