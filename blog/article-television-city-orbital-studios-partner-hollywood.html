A studio-lot LED volume is quietly becoming permanent Los Angeles infrastructure

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

The news is not that another virtual production stage opened. It is that real-time capture and R&D are moving into permanent studio infrastructure.

Television City, the historic studio lot at 7800 Beverly Blvd. in Los Angeles's Fairfax District, is adding a new LED volume virtual production facility, according to TV Tech. Hackman Capital bought the lot in 2018 for $750 million. The stage comes from Orbital Studios, founded in 2020 by A.J. Wedding, which is bringing camera tracking, real-time rendering, and an in-house Virtual Art Department to a full-service stage paired with an R&D lab. Orbital's roadmap spans AI-assisted content creation and location reconstruction through 3D Gaussian Splatting, and its recent credits include the docuseries 'World War II With Tom Hanks.'

The real signal is where the volume lives

The surface story is that another virtual production stage opened in Los Angeles. The real signal is where it opened and what came with it. A real-time capture stage and a working R&D lab are now permanent tenants on one of the city's landmark lots, not a rented tent that goes up for a single shoot and comes down the next week. When real-time rendering becomes fixed infrastructure on a $750 million property, it stops being an exotic line item negotiated per project and starts being part of the building's standing offer.

That shift changes the default. Camera tracking, a Virtual Art Department, and location reconstruction through Gaussian splats are being packaged as a standing service rather than a bespoke pipeline reassembled from scratch each time. For anyone commissioning content, the question quietly moves from 'can we afford a volume this time' to 'which volume, and what does its R&D lab let us prototype.' Add AI-assisted content creation to that roadmap and the stage starts to look less like a rental and more like a partner.

What a producer can do now

Treat a nearby LED volume as a reusable set, not a one-off build. Before greenlighting a location shoot, price the same environment as a real-time backdrop that can be relit, re-angled, and reused across a campaign's film, social cutdowns, and live moments. Ask whether the stage's R&D lab can reconstruct a real place as a Gaussian splat you own and revisit later. A stage with a lab attached is an invitation to test ideas, and Hollywood-grade virtual production is increasingly where brand and entertainment work share the same floor.

Why it belongs in an experiential toolkit

For experiential teams, a permanent volume is more than a film set. The same real-time environment that fills an LED wall can drive a projection surface, a headset walk-through, or a live show, because the underlying scene is a rendered world rather than flat footage. Keeping virtual production in the toolkit means an activation's hero environment can be authored once and re-deployed across screens, stages, and installations without being rebuilt for each format, which is usually where budgets leak. That reuse is the quiet economic case for keeping a volume within reach.

Your takeaway

Scope nearby LED volumes as reusable sets, and ask whether the stage's R&D lab can reconstruct real locations as Gaussian splats you own. One environment can then serve film, social, and live moments.

Why it matters

When real-time capture and R&D become permanent fixtures on a major lot, virtual production stops being an exotic add-on and starts being default infrastructure producers can plan around.
