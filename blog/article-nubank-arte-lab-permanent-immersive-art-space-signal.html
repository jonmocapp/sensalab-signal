A permanent immersive art venue opens on Avenida Paulista as fixed projection infrastructure

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

The news is not another projection show. It is a 2,700 square metre venue built to run immersive art as standing infrastructure, rebookable room by room.

ON, the audiovisual integrator, has completed the AV integration for Nubank Arte Lab, a permanent immersive art venue inside the Conjunto Nacional building on Avenida Paulista in Sao Paulo, which its partners describe as the country's largest permanent immersive art space, according to trade coverage from Essential Install and invidis. The venue occupies roughly 2,700 square metres and opened on 15 August 2026. ON's installation centres on 29 Barco WUXGA projectors across two immersive rooms, an 84 square metre transparent LED facade, a 3D LED sculpture built from 8,352 points and a mirrored infinity room. The two main rooms each measure about 23 by 9 metres, with projection covering three walls and the floor. The project was conceived by Aventura and Deeplab Project, developed with Nubank and designed by Jacobsen Arquitetura, and the space is built to be reconfigured for exhibitions, digital art, performances and corporate events.

The real signal is immersion as real estate

The surface story is a large new projection venue, and there are many of those. The real signal is permanence. A touring immersive show is a campaign with an end date; a fixed 2,700 square metre venue on one of Sao Paulo's main avenues is infrastructure that has to be filled, programmed and booked for years. That shifts the questions from how impressive the opening looks to how cheaply and quickly the rooms can be reset, and whether the fixed kit, projectors, LED facade and infinity room, can carry content no one has designed yet.

Naming a bank as the anchor is part of the signal too. When a financial brand puts its name on a permanent cultural venue rather than a one-off sponsorship, the space is expected to work as a long-run brand asset and a rentable room at once. The reconfiguration brief, exhibitions one week, a performance the next, a corporate event after that, is what makes the capital expenditure defensible.

What a producer can do now

If a client is tempted by a permanent immersive room, make reconfiguration the first line of the brief, not a nice-to-have. Ask what it costs in hours and money to turn the space over between a ticketed exhibition and a private event, because that number, not the projector count, decides whether the venue earns its lease. Treat the fixed hardware as a stage that has to accept content for years, and design the content pipeline alongside the build. Our activation kit is organised around exactly this: a space specified so the second and tenth show are as easy to stage as the first.

Why it belongs in an experiential toolkit

A permanent venue is the hardest version of immersive work because it removes the end date that forgives a thin concept. For a brand-experience lead, the transferable discipline is planning for the programming calendar before the ribbon is cut: fixed surfaces chosen for flexibility, rooms sized for more than one format, and an operating model that assumes the space will host work that does not exist yet.

Your takeaway

If you are briefing a permanent immersive room, specify for reconfiguration from day one. The projector count and LED surfaces matter less than whether the space can be reset for a new exhibition, a performance or a corporate night without a rebuild.

Why it matters

When a bank and a mall anchor a permanent immersive venue instead of a touring exhibition, immersion stops being an event you visit and becomes real estate that has to be programmed and booked.
