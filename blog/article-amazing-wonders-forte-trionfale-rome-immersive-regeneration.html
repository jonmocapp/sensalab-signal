Rome reopens a derelict fort with a temporary immersive show, not a renovation

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

The signal is the venue, not the medium. A disused military fort is reactivated through a ticketed, time-boxed immersive exhibition rather than a permanent rebuild.

Amazing Wonders, an international exhibition of immersive and digital art, opened on November 20, 2026 at Forte Trionfale in Rome and runs through April 30, 2027, with ticketing through Vivaticket. It spans 21,000 square metres and gathers more than 30 installations by 20 leading international artists and collectives, including six world premieres, among them a work by Random International made for the fort. The show is pitched explicitly at families and newcomers: it states that no prior knowledge of contemporary art or new technologies is required. Its venue is the story as much as its contents. Forte Trionfale is a former military fort, returned to the city after years of abandonment through a temporary urban regeneration project led by Urban Value.

The venue is the signal

The easy read is another large immersive art show. The sharper read is what the show is for. This is immersive content deployed as a reactivation tool: a disused fort that the city could not easily repurpose is opened to the public, temporarily, because a ticketed programme can justify the access, the staffing and the footfall. The exhibition is the reason the gates open, and the April end date is built in. The content is time-boxed because the regeneration is time-boxed.

That inverts the usual order. Most immersive shows look for a venue to fill. This one uses the show to unlock a venue that would otherwise stay dark. The accessibility framing is part of the same logic: curating for people with no art or tech background widens the audience, and a wide audience is what makes a temporary reactivation of 21,000 square metres pay for itself.

What a producer can do now

If you are scouting for a flagship space, look past purpose-built venues to the real estate nobody can currently use: forts, depots, halls and civic buildings stuck between owners or uses. A time-boxed, ticketed immersive programme can be the mechanism that reopens one of them, with a clear start and end that keeps the commitment contained. Curate for newcomers rather than insiders, because footfall, not prestige, is what justifies the fit-out. Plan the run as a temporary occupancy with defined dates, and treat the content as the thing that earns the access. A programme on this scale behaves like a touring show kit dropped into a found space rather than a gallery hang.

It is also why a venue reactivation belongs in an experiential toolkit and not only a cultural-programming plan. The exhibition is doing placemaking, footfall and audience development at once, inside a building that needed a reason to open. Making immersive content hold up across 21,000 square metres and five months is a show-control and operations problem as much as an artistic one.

Your takeaway

Treat immersive content as a way to unlock a venue, not just fill one: take a space that is otherwise unusable, run a time-boxed ticketed programme inside it, and curate for newcomers so accessibility drives the footfall that justifies the reactivation.

Why it matters

Amazing Wonders uses 30 immersive installations across 21,000 square metres to temporarily reopen a disused military fort in Rome, a sign immersive content is being used as a tool to reactivate dead civic real estate on a time-boxed, ticketed basis rather than to fill a purpose-built venue.
