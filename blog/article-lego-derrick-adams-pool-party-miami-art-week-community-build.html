LEGO lets Miami Art Week visitors assemble a nine-foot unicorn as the installation

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

The signal is a brand handing its centrepiece to the crowd. The product is not displayed finished. It is co-assembled in public over two days.

During Miami Art Week, artist Derrick Adams and the LEGO Group are staging Pool Party, an installation at Avant Gallery inside 1111 Lincoln Road in Miami Beach. Its centrepiece is a nine-foot unicorn pool float built entirely from LEGO bricks, set in a poolside landscape that includes roughly 3,000 LEGO Botanicals flowers. The key detail is that the unicorn is not presented finished: it is a community build over two days, December 3 and 4, 2026, from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily, with walk-ins welcome and advance registration encouraged. The unicorn motif comes from Adams's own work, growing out of a playground installation he created for the Faena Hotel in Miami and his longer-running series of inflatable pool floats.

The build is the content

The real signal is what gets handed over. The usual brand move at an art fair is a finished, lit, roped-off object engineered to photograph. Here the hero object arrives incomplete on purpose, and the crowd finishes it. The product stops being something you look at and becomes something you do, over a defined window, in a space you can walk into. That reframes the activation from display to participation, and it changes what success looks like: not a single reveal, but two days of people with their hands in it.

It also lowers the barrier deliberately. Walk-ins and daytime hours signal that the point is throughput and participation, not an exclusive guest list. The unfinished unicorn gives every visitor a reason to touch the brand's material rather than admire it from a distance, and the slow public assembly turns dwell time into the story people carry out and share.

What a producer can do now

If your product or material can be assembled, consider making the assembly the event rather than hiding it. Choose a hero object big enough to anchor a room, stage it as a public build over a fixed window, and keep entry low-friction so participation scales. Design for hands-on contact and for the photograph of people building, not only the finished piece. A co-created centrepiece is harder to control than a sculpture that arrives done, so plan the kit, the stations and the staffing as carefully as the final look. This is the shape of a strong brand activation kit: a clear hero, a simple way in, and a reason to stay and make something.

That is also why a participatory build belongs in an experiential toolkit and not just a fabrication brief. The unicorn is doing brand contact, dwell and content generation at once, and the build itself is the mechanism. Getting that to run cleanly across two public days is an operations and show problem as much as a design one.

Your takeaway

When the product can be assembled, make the assembly the event: let visitors build a hero object in public over a fixed window, keep entry low-friction with walk-ins, and let the act of making it carry the brand rather than a finished reveal.

Why it matters

Derrick Adams and the LEGO Group are building a nine-foot unicorn float as a two-day public assembly rather than a finished display during Miami Art Week, a sign brands are staging product as a participatory, co-created act instead of a polished object to photograph.
