teamLab drawing 1.3 million to one Kyoto museum signals immersive art as destination

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

The story is not the headcount. It is a permanent immersive venue pulling international visitors the way a landmark does.

teamLab Biovortex Kyoto has marked its first anniversary, reporting more than 1.3 million visitors from 167 countries and regions in its opening year, according to My Modern Met and teamLab. International guests made up about 44 percent of attendance, drawn heavily from the United States, Australia, Canada, the United Kingdom and Germany. The museum, which opened in October 2025, had already passed 500,000 visitors within five months and one million within nine. Kyoto's mayor, Koji Matsui, issued a congratulatory message, and to mark the milestone the museum is showing a fasciated willow within its artwork Vanishing Tree and Wildflowers from 7 to 11 October.

The signal is the passport count, not the attendance

A million-plus visitors is a striking number, but the detail that carries the signal is the 167 countries and the 44 percent of guests who travelled from abroad. That is not a local attraction with good word of mouth; it is a permanent immersive venue functioning as a reason to visit a city. The real move is immersive art behaving like destination infrastructure, an anchor that pulls international footfall on its own, the way a landmark museum or a stadium does, rather than a seasonal show that depends on a nearby crowd.

The ramp matters too. Half a million in five months, a million in nine, 1.3 million in twelve: that is steady demand across a full year, not a launch spike that fades. A venue that holds its draw once the opening press has moved on is a different proposition from a pop-up. It is a content asset with a long life, which changes how much it is worth investing in the work that fills it and how often that work should change.

What a producer can do now

The lesson for anyone planning a brand or civic experience is to design for return and reach, not for an opening weekend. If a space is meant to pull people from other countries, the work inside has to reward the trip and the repeat visit, which means budgeting for a canvas that evolves rather than a fixed install. A considered activations kit plans the content lifecycle up front, what changes, how often, and what keeps a permanent space worth returning to, so the experience earns its draw over years rather than a season.

It belongs in an experiential toolkit because the same logic now applies to brand spaces. As flagships, brand homes and immersive rooms are asked to behave like destinations, the measure shifts from launch traffic to sustained, international pull. Designing a space people cross a border to see, and come back to, is a higher bar than a photogenic opening, and it is the bar more permanent brand experiences are now being held to.
