Build-A-Bear building a three-story Orlando workshop signals retail rebuilt as a destination

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

The story is not a bigger store. It is a maker brand turning a flagship into an attraction people route a trip around.

Build-A-Bear is opening its largest-ever workshop, a multi-story experiential store at Orlando's ICON Park, with a facade on International Drive, according to Retail TouchPoints. The location introduces the first-ever Build-A-Bear Design Studio, where guests book an appointment and work one-on-one with a design consultant, alongside an interactive Bakeshop the company describes as a dessert destination that extends beyond the make-your-own core, and a Personalize Me station. The design firm is Chute Gerdeman, which also worked on the FAO Schwarz New York shop-in-shop. The company has framed the concept as retail-tainment and said further features and grand-opening details would follow.

The signal is the pacing, not the square footage

A bigger store is not a story on its own. The detail that carries the signal is how the space is organised: a booked, one-to-one Design Studio, an interactive Bakeshop, a personalisation station. Those are not fixtures, they are stations in a sequence, each one a made moment with its own dwell time. The brand is not scaling a sales floor; it is pacing a visit the way an attraction paces a queue and a ride. The real move is maker-retail being designed as a destination people route a trip around, where the measure is time spent and reason to travel, not transactions per square foot.

Placing it at ICON Park, among Orlando's tourist attractions rather than in a conventional mall, confirms the intent. A flagship sited next to paid attractions is competing for a visitor's day, not an errand. That only works if the store delivers a sequence worth the detour, which is why the design leans on booked and interactive moments rather than shelves.

What a producer can do now

For anyone designing brand spaces, the lesson is to plan the journey before the fixtures. A flagship built as a destination needs a defined sequence of moments, each with a purpose and a pace, rather than a floor plan that happens to be large. That means mapping what a guest does minute to minute, where the one-to-one and interactive beats land, and how the space holds attention between them, before deciding what goes on the walls. The stores that pull a trip are the ones authored as experiences, not merchandised as rooms.

That is why it belongs in an experiential toolkit. As more brands ask their flagships to behave like attractions, the discipline shifts from visual merchandising to experience design, from arranging product to pacing a visit. A disciplined retail kit scopes that journey up front, the made moments, the interactive and real-time layers, and how they sustain dwell time, so a flagship earns the trip rather than hoping footfall converts.

Your takeaway

Design a flagship around a sequence of made moments, not a sales floor: when the store is paced like an attraction with booked, one-to-one and interactive stations, dwell time and reason-to-travel become the metrics, so plan the journey before the fixtures.
