The AI backlash made presence the premium

Edition 19·18 Jul 2026·3 min read

As AI-generated everything floods every feed, the thing people will stop for, and pay for, is the moment that could only happen once, in a room, with them in it. That is a market signal worth planning around.

Audiences are getting tired of synthetic everything, and it is showing up as a premium on the real. As AI-generated images, voices, and video flood every feed, the thing people will stop for, and pay for, is presence: a live brand experience that could only happen once, in a room, with them in it. That is not nostalgia. It is a market signal worth planning around.

This is not an argument against AI. It is a signal about what stays scarce.

Plenty of the work behind a great experience will be AI-assisted, and should be. The point is not to reject the tools. The point is what they do to value. When anything can be generated on demand, generated content stops being scarce, and scarce is what commands attention and price. What cannot be mass-produced is a moment that happens live, in a specific place, for the specific people who showed up.

That is the whole thesis of experiential work, and the backlash just made it obvious. Anything that can be generated can be generated again, endlessly, for anyone. A moment that happened once, in one room, for the people who were actually there, cannot. Scarcity is the whole difference, and presence is the scarcest thing left to sell.

Why do live experiences still beat AI content?

Because some things only exist when people are in the same place at the same time. A synthetic clip can be watched a million times, alone, on a million screens, and it never once makes a room react together. That shared, simultaneous response needs bodies present to exist at all. When synthetic content is infinite and nearly free, the premium moves to the moment that was real and unrepeatable.

Presence is the product AI cannot fake.

So build for the thing the technology cannot copy. Picture the beat when a reveal lands and a whole crowd catches its breath at the same instant, or the second a stranger turns to the person beside them because they both just saw it happen. That reaction is shared, simultaneous, and impossible to generate, because it only exists when people are genuinely in the room. You can measure the pull of it, too, without inventing numbers: the presence premium, what people will give up to be there, the travel, the queue, the ticket price they will pay, and whether they return. Those are the case you take to a finance lead who has seen enough synthetic video to be skeptical of one more.

So draw the line before the next brief lands. When everything can be generated, presence is the premium. Put your budget where the value is getting scarcer, in the live, physical, participatory moment, and let AI speed up the production behind it rather than replace the point of it.
