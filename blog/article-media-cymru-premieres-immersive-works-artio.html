Media Cymru at Outernet: the signal in regional studios shipping immersive at scale

Edition 21·02 Aug 2026·3 min read

The news is not two Welsh art pieces in London. It is regional production clusters reaching a 6-million-visitor stage on their own terms and their own data.

Media Cymru is premiering two Welsh-made immersive works at Outernet London, with a launch event on 4 March 2026 and public showings from March 2026. Artio is an interactive Arthurian musical mythscape that uses gesture recognition and generative visuals. Awen: Unbound uses light, sound and real Welsh environmental data, with plant-generated music. Outernet London draws more than 6 million visitors a year, making it the city's most-visited cultural attraction.

The real signal is where the work comes from

The surface story is two art pieces on a big screen. The structural story is more interesting: a regionally funded production cluster is putting work on a stage that sees more than 6 million visitors a year, in another country, on its own creative and technical terms. Gesture recognition, generative visuals, and a piece scored by real environmental data are not entry-level techniques. That capability now lives outside the usual production capitals, and it is booking into the highest-footfall venues on merit.

That matters because it changes the map of who you can work with. For years the assumption was that venue-grade immersive talent clustered in a handful of cities. A funded regional ecosystem shipping to Outernet says the opposite: the pool of studios that can deliver at scale is wider, and often carries something the capitals cannot, such as specific local data, IP, or a cultural frame, like Welsh environmental readings driving plant-generated music. The differentiator is not just the tech; it is the material only that place can supply.

What a producer can do now

Widen your scouting. When you need an immersive collaborator, the regionally funded cluster is now a serious source, sometimes with public co-funding that improves the economics, and usually with a distinctive angle you cannot buy off the shelf. Just as usefully, watch Outernet itself as a reference site: a piece that holds 6 million passers-by a year is a live case study in what reads at scale and what gets ignored. Both the partners and the venue are data points worth logging before your next brief.

For a toolkit, the through-line is that gesture, generative visuals, and live data feeds are becoming standard immersive ingredients rather than exotic ones. Being fluent in interactive, engine-driven worlds, where a visitor's movement or a live data stream drives what they see in real time, is what turns a Welsh dataset scoring the music from a one-off feat into a technique you can propose to any client with a story and a signal to bind together.

You do not need to be in a production capital, or to staff a real-time team, to put work like this in front of an audience. Your takeaway

When scouting immersive partners, look past the usual capitals. Regionally funded clusters are shipping venue-grade work, often with distinctive data or IP you cannot get elsewhere.

Why it matters

High-footfall venues are now accessible to regionally funded work, which widens the pool of collaborators and reference-grade sites producers can actually book.
