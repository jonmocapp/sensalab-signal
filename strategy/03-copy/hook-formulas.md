# Hook formulas — hero openers for INMERSIVO

Reusable formulas for the hero of each edition (headline, sub, and the big `statement`).
These are the shapes behind the lines that already work in editions 05 and 06; the engine's
writer can instantiate them with each week's sourced material.

## The house pattern: the two-beat statement

Every hero (and every section `statement`) wants the same skeleton:

```
Beat 1 (setup): a concrete thing that happened, stated flat.  ≤ 45 chars ideal.
Beat 2 (turn):  the reframe, consequence, or verdict.          ≤ 45 chars ideal.
```

Two sentences or one sentence with a hard turn. No em dashes. The turn must be earned by
the sources, never invented. Live examples already in the sims:

- "They sanded the grime off Shrek. **The audience called it fake.**"
- "Harry Potter didn't reopen a movie. **It opened a room.**"
- "Fans didn't want seats. **They wanted a job.**"

## 10 formulas

### 1. The reframe — "[Familiar spectacle] is just [buildable system]"
Collapses awe into something the producer can commission. Best for **In the Lab**.
- "A scoreboard in the sky is just a data pipeline with an audience."
- Template: "A [wow thing] is just a [system] with [an audience / a venue / a budget]."
- Guardrail: the system named must be literally true of the case.

### 2. The verdict — "They [did X]. The audience [did Y]"
Cause and public consequence, no editorializing needed. Best for **Teardown** heroes.
- "They sanded the grime off Shrek. The audience called it fake."
- Template: "They [tactile verb + detail]. The [jury] called it [verdict word]."
- The tactile verb (sanded, scrubbed, flattened, smoothed) carries the craft argument.

### 3. The promotion — "[Audience/format] just became [new role]"
Declares a status change that raises the reader's stakes. Best for **hero** lines.
- "Your audience just became a craft critic."
- Template: "[Your audience / the watch party / the back catalog] just became [a critic / a venue / inventory]."

### 4. The negation — "The best of [event] wasn't [the obvious thing]"
Contrarian read of a story the reader thinks they already know. Best for **Signal** heroes
tied to a mega-event.
- "The best of the World Cup happened outside the stadiums."
- Template: "The best [X] at [event] wasn't [the expected X]."

### 5. The compressed triad — "[N] [unit], [N] [unit], one [payoff]"
Numbers do the hooking; the third slot flips to value. Numbers must be sourced.
- "400 drones, 12 minutes, one idea worth stealing."
- "Sixty seconds, three stolen ideas." (video slot)
- Template: "[sourced N] [noun], [sourced N] [noun], one [lesson / steal / principle]."

### 6. The job statement — "The ones that worked gave [audience] a job"
Participation thesis; behavior as proof. Best for **Field Notes**.
- "The activations people queued for handed them a ball, a game, a role."
- Template: "The [category] people [queued for / paid for / came back to] gave them [a role]."

### 7. The aphorism — "[X] isn't [Y]" (≤ 5 words)
A craft law, stated like it was always true. Use once per edition, max.
- "Polish isn't finish." · "Texture is trust." · "Watching is table stakes."
- Template: "[Virtue everyone chases] isn't [the thing that matters]."

### 8. The grown-up — "[Humble format] grew up. It [proof] now"
Category maturation; the proof beat must be concrete behavior (tickets, budgets, queues).
- "The watch party grew up. It sells tickets now."
- Template: "The [small format] grew up. It [sells tickets / fills venues / has a budget line] now."

### 9. The material reframe — "[Live input] is [design material] now"
Turns a data source into scenography; speaks straight to producers' toolkits.
- "Live data is set design now."
- Template: "[Scores / votes / crowds / weather] is [set design / scenography / a canvas] now."

### 10. The loss mechanism — "Remove the [detail], remove the [belief]"
Names what dies when craft is deleted. Best for **Craft** sections and Teardown principles.
- "Remove the fingerprints, remove the belief."
- Template: "Remove the [scuff / grime / weight / friction], remove the [belief / place / person]."

## Matching formula to slot

| Slot | First choice | Second choice |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Hero (Signal) | 4 negation | 3 promotion |
| Hero (Teardown) | 2 verdict | 3 promotion |
| Field Notes statement | 6 job statement | 2 verdict |
| In the Lab statement | 1 reframe | 9 material reframe |
| Craft statement | 10 loss mechanism | 8 grown-up |
| "Why it matters" line | 7 aphorism | 10 loss mechanism |
| Video statement | 5 compressed triad | — |

## Rules that apply to every formula

1. Sourced facts only in the setup beat; opinion lives in the turn.
2. No em dashes, no exclamation points, sentence case.
3. If the statement repeats the headline, one of the two is wrong (usually the statement:
   it should compress, not echo).
4. The "why" line is a separate organism: ≤ 8 words, one principle, no restating the body.
5. Never point the hook at SensaLab. The hook is about the market; the association does
   the selling (brief: expert by association, no hard sell).
