# Subject lines & preheaders — INMERSIVO

Specialist 03 deliverable. Everything below is in English, sentence case, and fits the
engine contract: **subject ≤ 60 chars, preheader (`preview_text`) ≤ 90 chars**.
No em dashes, no exclamation points, no all-caps, no invented numbers — every figure
below comes from the sourced editions (2 million, 400 drones, 12 minutes, 87-foot, 40
matches, 12K, 3v3, 4,000 photos, 25-year-old).

Psychological angles use this shorthand:
- **curiosity gap** — opens a loop the email must close
- **contrarian** — flips the expected read of a known event
- **reframe** — renames a familiar thing as something more useful
- **utility** — promises a steal, a playbook, ammunition
- **identity** — speaks to the producer's role, status, or fear of being behind
- **loss / threat** — names what it costs to ignore this
- **future shock** — declares that the ground already moved
- **specificity** — a concrete detail does the persuading

## 5 A/B test pairs (one variable each)

Run one pair per send, 50/50 split, judge on open rate after 24h, click rate as guardrail
metric (a subject that wins opens but tanks clicks over-promised). Log winners: after 4-6
tests the bank should be rebalanced toward the winning angle family.

| Pair | A | B | Variable tested | Hypothesis |
|------|---|---|-----------------|------------|
| 1 | S1 The best of the World Cup happened off the pitch | S2 Steal these 3 ideas before your next pitch | Insight-frame vs. utility-frame | Producers open for ammunition ("steal") over analysis; if B wins, lead the bank with utility. |
| 2 | S3 A scoreboard in the sky is just a data pipeline | S15 Live data is set design now | Long-specific vs. short-abstract reframe | Specificity beats compression for a cold-ish B2B list; if B wins, the list rewards confidence. |
| 3 | S19 Before your client asks about domes | S10 Your client watched the final in an 87-foot dome | Anticipation vs. fait accompli | Fear of being unprepared (A) outpulls fear of being behind (B) for senior producers. |
| 4 | T1 They sanded the grime off Shrek | T16 The flaw everyone felt but nobody named | Named case vs. blind curiosity gap | The named IP wins on recognition; if T16 wins, curiosity beats recognition and future teardowns can tease the case. |
| 5 | T2 Polish isn't finish | T5 One teaser, comments off | Principle vs. concrete consequence | Aphorisms flatter an expert audience; if T5 wins, the list wants events first, laws second. |
