Teardown #09 · 27.07.2026
The teardown

The money showed up. The interactivity didn't.

Experiential spending is at a record. Most of what it buys is still a photo-op.

Case · The $128B experiential boom visual — capa de arte

The money showed up. The interactivity didn't.

The category is winning the budget war. It is losing the participation war.

The flaw

Global experiential spend passed 128 billion dollars in 2024 — its first time above pre-pandemic levels — and 51% of brands say they'll spend more through 2026. But walk the floor and most 'immersive' activations are the same thing dressed up: a branded backdrop, a hashtag, a queue for a photo. That is reach wearing a costume, not participation.

The principle

The activations that convert are multi-sensory and responsive — they change based on what the visitor does. A spectacle you only watch is expensive reach. An experience that answers you is a relationship.

Why it mattersThe budget is already moving toward experiential. The brands that make it respond — not just look good — are the ones that turn footfall into a line on the pipeline.
Meanwhile, done right

The World Cup 2026 activations that landed weren't the biggest screens — they were the ones that gave fans something to do. U.S. World Cup experiences are reportedly up 403% versus 2022, with attendance up 572%. Demand isn't the problem. Passivity is.
